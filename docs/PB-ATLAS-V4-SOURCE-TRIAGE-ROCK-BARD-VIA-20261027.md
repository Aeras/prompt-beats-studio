## ru.russian_rock_80 / ru.rock_70
**A** A. Zaytseva, “The Legitimation of Rock Music in the Soviet Union in the 1970s and 1980s,” *Cahiers du monde russe* 49(4), 651–680, 2008, DOI 10.4000/monderusse.9500, https://shs.cairn.info/article/E_CMR_494_0651?lang=en (publisher abstract).
- Narrow evidence: legitimization of Soviet/Russian rock during late 1970s/1980s, the Leningrad new wave and institutions; cultural association with poetic traditions, bard songs and urban folklore; no universal meter, chord progression, instruments, song section template or narrative prescription.
**B** Yngvar B. Steinholt, “You can't rid a song of its words: notes on the hegemony of lyrics in Russian rock songs,” *Popular Music* 22(1), 89–108 (2003), DOI 10.1017/S0261143003003064, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/popular-music/article/abs/you-cant-rid-a-song-of-its-words-notes-on-the-hegemony-of-lyrics-in-russian-rock-songs/9283A28D523ED83DBBA0F314149FDA48 (publisher abstract).
- Narrow evidence: cultural and critical emphasis on literary/lyric qualities; the article explicitly questions a purely literary approach. Do not misrepresent attention to words as proof that *all* rock songs must be lyric-centric or contain political themes.
Candidate EL: «Στη σοβιετική/ρωσική ροκ σκηνή των ύστερων 1970s και 1980s, μελέτες αναδεικνύουν τον ρόλο θεσμών, ποιητικών αναφορών και της πρόσληψης των στίχων· πρόκειται για ιστορικό και κριτικό πλαίσιο, όχι καθολικό ηχητικό κανόνα.»

Status: unvalidated hypothesis, executable=false.

## ru.via / ru.soviet_estrada_70 / ru.soviet_estrada_80
**E** Christiane Schäfer, “Institution, Memory and a Brief Flirtation: An Introduction into Soviet Vocal-Instrumental Ensembles,” *Apparatus* 13 (2021), DOI 10.17892/app.2021.00013.273, https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/273 (publisher abstract; publicly available PDF at https://apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/download/273/547 ).
- VIA are an *ensemble/institutional format*, with a broad musical spectrum and cultural/geographical variation across the Soviet Union; an essay about VIA cannot establish a universal “1970s Estrada sound”.
**F** Clemens Günther and Christiane Schäfer, “An Open Invitation: VIA as a Field of Research,” *Apparatus* 13 (2021), DOI 10.17892/app.2021.00013.277, https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/277 (publisher abstract).
- Records success from mid-1960s to mid-1980s and multifaceted cultural/institutional roles; does not prove a given synthesizer, brass section, chord sequence or BPM for all examples.
Candidate EL: «Τα VIA αποτελούν ιστορική μορφή φωνητικο-οργανικών συνόλων με ευρύ και πολυεθνικό σοβιετικό ρεπερτόριο· δεν αποτελούν ένα ενιαίο υποείδος με υποχρεωτικό ηχητικό πρότυπο.»
Candidate EN: “VIA denotes a historical vocal-instrumental ensemble format spanning diverse Soviet repertories; it is not a single subgenre with a mandatory sonic template.”
Creative experiment only: Invite selection of a specific source ensemble/recording before borrowing an optional vocal-instrumental balance; absent such source, avoid imposing presumed ‘Soviet vintage’ sound. Status unvalidated, executable=false.
